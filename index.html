LLM Deep Dive:
From First Principles

From tokenization to modern architecture variants — the algorithms, math, and data behind large language models

This course is for engineers who want to look under the hood and understand how LLMs actually work. We’ll move beyond treating models as black boxes and examine the exact mechanisms — from how text gets turned into numbers to the linear algebra inside attention layers — that make these systems possible.

The "LLM Math Primer" gave you the vocabulary. This course gives you the engineering — the actual algorithms, data structures, and mathematical machinery that make LLMs work. Each lesson combines symbolic notation, architectural diagrams, interactive charts, and real data to build a grounded understanding of how modern LLMs are built, trained, compressed, and deployed.
TL;DR

8 lessons covering the core technical foundations of modern LLMs: Lesson 1 — how tokenization and embeddings turn text into vectors; Lesson 2 — the full transformer architecture with attention math; Lesson 3 — pretraining objectives, data pipelines, and loss dynamics; Lesson 4 — quantization techniques (INT8, NF4, GPTQ) and model compression; Lesson 5 — inference optimization (KV cache, speculative decoding, batching); Lesson 6 — scaling laws with empirical data; Lesson 7 — RLHF, DPO, and instruction tuning; Lesson 8 — modern innovations (MoE, Mamba, GQA, RoPE, FlashAttention).

Syllabus

Lesson 1: Tokenization & Embeddings

How raw text becomes numerical representations. We'll explore Byte-Pair Encoding, WordPiece, SentencePiece tokenizers, learned embeddings, and positional encodings (sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi). Plus, we'll see why tokenization choices matter with real-world examples.

Lesson 2: The Transformer Architecture

Complete architecture breakdown with symbolic notation. Scaled dot-product attention, multi-head attention, encoder vs decoder vs encoder-decoder. Layer norm, residual connections, FFN equations. Plus, we'll visualize the data flow and see why each component exists.

Lesson 3: Training & Pretraining

Pretraining objectives (causal LM, masked LM, permutation LM). Data curation pipelines, deduplication, quality filtering. Training dynamics — loss curves, learning rate schedules, batch size scaling. Compute budgets and training costs. We'll also look at emergent abilities and scaling behaviors.

Lesson 4: Quantization & Model Compression

Why quantization works. INT8, INT4, NF4 formats. Post-training quantization (GPTQ, AWQ) vs quantization-aware training. Impact on model size, quality, and inference speed. Interactive chart comparing precision/quality/size trade-offs. We'll also discuss when quantization hurts and how to mitigate it.

Lesson 5: Inference & Deployment

The inference stack: KV cache mechanism, memory bandwidth wall, speculative decoding, continuous batching, prefix caching. Throughput vs latency trade-offs. GPU memory budgeting for production serving. Plus, we'll see real-world latency numbers and optimization strategies.

Lesson 6: Scaling Laws

Kaplan et al. and Chinchilla scaling laws with empirical data. Power-law relationships between compute/data/parameters/performance. Optimal allocation for compute budgets. Interactive scaling law chart with real data points. We'll also discuss the implications for model design and the limits of scaling.

Lesson 7: Alignment & Instruction Tuning

From pretraining to helpful assistant. Instruction tuning datasets, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). RLHF — reward modeling, PPO. DPO — direct preference optimization. Constitutional AI and the alignment tax. We'll also look at practical alignment techniques and failure modes.

Lesson 8: Modern Architecture Variants

Beyond the vanilla transformer. Mixture of Experts (MoE) — routing, load balancing, expert capacity. Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), Multi-Query Attention (MQA). RoPE embeddings. Mamba — state space models for linear-time inference. FlashAttention — IO-aware exact attention. We'll also discuss when to choose which variant.
